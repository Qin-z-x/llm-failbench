# Code of Conduct

FailBench is a technical, evidence-first project. This code applies to issues,
pull requests, reviews, discussions, and any other project communication. It is
short by design: it is a working agreement for people who want to build a
useful benchmark together, not a legal document.

## Expectations

1. Be respectful. Treat other contributors as professionals and discuss ideas
   on their merits.
2. No harassment, discrimination, personal attacks, or sustained hostility.
3. Critique evidence and code, not people. Evidence over assertion: when you
   disagree, cite the source, probe, or run that supports your position.
4. Assume good faith, and correct errors in public with sources rather than by
   disparaging the person who made them.
5. No fabricated data, metrics, or sources. If you cannot verify something, say
   so instead of guessing.
6. No harmful payloads and no personal data: no working jailbreaks, exploit or
   injection payloads aimed at third parties, malware, PII, or private or
   leaked data.
7. Respect licenses and attribution. Code is MIT and data is CC-BY-4.0; do not
   copy long text, and cite sources with a link and a short quote.
8. Keep discussions on-topic and technical. Move unrelated disputes elsewhere.
9. Maintainers may moderate, edit, or remove off-topic, abusive, or unsafe
   content and contributions.
10. Report concerns by opening an issue or contacting a maintainer through the
    repository's issue tracker.

## Enforcement

Maintainers will review reports of violations and may warn, edit, or remove
content and contributions, or restrict participation, as proportionate to the
situation. Good-faith mistakes are correctable: a corrected attribution, a
removed metric, or an honest note about an unverified claim is a positive
outcome, and the project prefers correction over punishment.

Reports are handled as confidentially as the project's tooling permits. No
specific response time or outcome is promised; maintainers are volunteers.
Retaliation against someone who raises a concern in good faith is itself a
violation of this code.
